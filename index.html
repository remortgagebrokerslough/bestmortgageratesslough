What Is a Commercial Mortgage?

A commercial mortgage is a loan secured against a non-residential property, used either to purchase premises a business will trade from, to acquire an investment property that will be let to tenants, or to refinance an existing asset to release capital or improve terms. Unlike residential lending, commercial mortgages are individually underwritten: every application is assessed on the strength of the business, the quality of the property, and the realistic exit route, rather than matched against a single standardised product.

Types of Commercial Mortgages Available in Slough

Owner-occupied commercial mortgages

For businesses that want to stop paying rent and buy the premises they trade from — a warehouse unit, a retail shop, an office suite, a workshop — an owner-occupied mortgage is typically secured against the trading business's accounts, its sector, and the resale or re-letting appeal of the property itself.

Commercial investment mortgages

Investors purchasing property to let to a third-party tenant, whether a single let industrial unit or a parade of shops, are assessed primarily on rental income against the loan repayments, tenant covenant strength, and lease length remaining.

Semi-commercial and mixed-use mortgages

Many properties around Slough combine a ground-floor shop or unit with residential flats above. These semi-commercial assets sit between residential buy-to-let and full commercial lending, and need a lender comfortable underwriting both elements of the property together.

Development and bridging finance

Short-term funding is often used to acquire a site, convert a building, or bridge the gap between purchase and a longer-term mortgage once a property is refurbished, let, or ready for refinance onto a standard commercial term.

How the Commercial Mortgage Process Works

The process usually begins with a conversation about the property, the business, and the intended use of funds, followed by a review of trading accounts, bank statements and, where relevant, a business plan or cash flow forecast. From there, the broker matches the case to suitable lenders, secures an initial decision in principle, and coordinates the property valuation and legal work through to completion. Keeping accounts up to date and being clear about the purpose of the loan from the outset are two of the simplest ways to keep the process moving quickly.

What Lenders Look At

  • Deposit available and the resulting loan-to-value, typically between 65% and 75% for standard commercial purchases
  • Trading history, profitability and affordability of the business or, for investment property, the rental income against repayments
  • The type, condition and location of the property, including how easily it could be re-let or resold
  • The experience of the applicant in the relevant sector or property type
  • A clear exit strategy, particularly for short-term development or bridging finance

Rates, Terms and Fees

Commercial mortgage pricing is rarely a single published rate. It reflects the loan-to-value, the strength of the covenant, the property type, and the term requested, with both fixed and variable structures available depending on the lender. Arrangement fees, valuation costs and legal fees are also part of the overall cost of the deal, and a broker's role includes setting these out clearly from the outset so there are no surprises later in the process.

Choosing the Right Broker in Slough

Look for a broker who works across the whole market rather than a single panel of lenders, who can point to recent experience with property types similar to yours, and who is transparent about fees, timelines and the realistic likelihood of approval before any application is submitted. A good broker will also be candid about which lenders are unlikely to be a fit, saving time that would otherwise be spent on applications that were never going to succeed.
